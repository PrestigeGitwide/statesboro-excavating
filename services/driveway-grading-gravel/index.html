Why Adding Stone Alone May Not Last

Fresh gravel can fill a hole temporarily, but a flat or poorly drained lane may collect water in the same place again. Where conditions allow, grading reclaims loose aggregate, reshapes the surface, and gives runoff a shorter path toward a stable outlet before new material is considered.

Soft subgrade, an undersized outlet, or runoff entering from adjacent ground may require more than surface maintenance. In those cases the project can connect to site grading or drainage work.

Reworking the Existing Drive

Where enough usable aggregate remains, it can often be loosened, redistributed, and reshaped instead of immediately buried. Edges and entrances receive different traffic and water than the center of a long lane, so the full route should be evaluated rather than treating every depression the same.

Some failures extend into the base or subgrade and cannot be solved by surface grading. We will distinguish routine maintenance from deeper reconstruction needs when the condition is visible.

Driveway grading is especially important for rural properties, longer driveways, farm access roads, and areas with drainage challenges.

Choosing and Spreading Additional Aggregate

Material selection should match availability, existing stone, vehicle loads, and whether the project needs base repair or a driving surface. Common local options may include graded aggregate, granite products, or recycled material, but availability and suitability should be confirmed for each job.

Aggregate quantity is based on area, intended depth, and compaction—not simply the number of truckloads used on a previous property. Delivery and spreading responsibilities are clarified in the estimate.

Keep Runoff From Traveling Down the Lane

Water moving lengthwise gains speed and can carry fines and stone away. A crown or cross-slope can help shed water, but only when the shoulder and outlet give that water somewhere appropriate to go.

Frequently Asked Questions About Driveway Grading and Gravel Services

How often should a gravel driveway be graded?

This depends on traffic, weather, drainage, and the condition of the driveway. Many gravel driveways benefit from periodic grading to maintain a smoother surface and improve drainage.

Can grading fix potholes and washouts?

In many cases, yes. Grading can help smooth potholes, reshape the driveway surface, and improve water runoff to reduce future damage.

Do you spread new gravel?

Yes. We can spread and level gravel for driveway resurfacing, new driveways, parking areas, and access roads depending on the project needs.

Can you work on long rural driveways?

Absolutely. We regularly work on farm lanes, acreage driveways, rural access roads, and longer gravel surfaces throughout Bulloch County.

Can driveway grading help with drainage issues?

Yes. Proper grading helps direct water away from the driveway surface, which can reduce erosion, potholes, standing water, and washouts.
